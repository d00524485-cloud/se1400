Collectors

Start a collection that lasts.

Provenance is written for people who care about where their clothes came from. Here is how to start collecting with a plan, and how to share your own closet with our readers.

Five habits of good collectors

  1. Pick a focus. One designer, one era or one type of garment. A focused collection is easier to learn, easier to authenticate and usually worth more as a whole.
  2. Learn before you spend. Read runway reviews and look through old lookbooks so you know which seasons and pieces matter.
  3. Buy condition, not just names. A clean, common piece is often a better buy than a rare one that is badly damaged.
  4. Keep the paper trail. Save receipts, tags and listing screenshots. Proof of where a piece came from is part of its value.
  5. Wear it. Clothes were made to be worn. Rotate pieces, store them well, and enjoy them.

Caring for archive pieces

Hang heavy coats and leather on wide, shaped hangers so the shoulders keep their form. Fold knits instead of hanging them. Keep silver in a dry, sealed bag to slow tarnish, and have delicate pieces cleaned by a specialist rather than washing them at home.

Where to find pieces

Most archive pieces change hands on resale marketplaces such as Grailed, through Japanese auction and proxy-buying services, and in small vintage shops. Each has trade-offs. Marketplaces offer buyer protection but higher prices, Japanese auctions can be cheaper but add shipping and fees, and shops let you try things on in person. Whichever you use, run every piece through our authentication checklist and compare the price with recent sales before you commit.
