Authentication

How to spot a fake.

Counterfeits of archive pieces are getting better every year. No single check proves a piece is real, but working through the same steps every time catches most problems before you pay.

  1. LookStudy the listing photos closely.
  2. CompareMatch details against verified pieces.
  3. QuestionAsk the seller for anything missing.
  4. DecideWalk away if things do not add up.

What to check

Open each section for details. These checks apply to most labels we cover.

Labels and wash-care tags

Tags are the first place counterfeiters cut corners. Check the font, spacing and stitching on the main label, and make sure the wash-care tag lists materials and a country of manufacture that match the season. Maison Margiela, for example, uses a plain white label attached by four visible white stitches, and fakes often get the stitch placement wrong.

Stitching and construction

Real archive pieces were made carefully. Look for even stitch length, clean seam finishes inside the garment, and linings that sit flat. Loose threads, crooked seams and cheap interior fabric are warning signs, especially on pieces with high resale prices.

Hardware and silver

Zippers, buttons and snaps should feel heavy and be branded where the label normally brands them. Chrome Hearts silver is sterling and is usually stamped with a .925 mark. Compare engraving depth and crisp edges against verified photos.

Seller history and listing details

Check how long the seller has been active, what else they have sold, and their feedback. Be cautious of stock photos, prices far below market, and sellers who refuse to send extra pictures. Pay through a platform that offers buyer protection.
